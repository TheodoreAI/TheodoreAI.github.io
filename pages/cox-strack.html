Cox & Strack Test Structure

A transmission line measurement (TLM) structure used to extract specific contact resistance at a metal-semiconductor interface — the technique behind my undergraduate thesis on heterojunction solar cell contact resistance.

How it works

Circular metal contacts of increasing radius are deposited on top of a semiconductor layer, each electrically isolated by mesa etching down to the substrate. Current is forced between pairs of contacts and the resulting voltage is measured. Because each contact has a different radius (and therefore a different contact-to-substrate resistance path), plotting resistance against contact geometry lets you separate the sheet resistance of the semiconductor from the specific contact resistance at the metal interface — the quantity the Cox & Strack method solves for.
