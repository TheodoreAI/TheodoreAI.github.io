How it works

My thesis compares two ways to extract specific contact resistance (Rc) at a metal-semiconductor interface. Use the toggle above the scene to switch between them.

TLM — total resistance between two adjacent contacts follows

RT = 2Rc + (Rsh/W)·d

where d is the spacing between contacts, W is the contact width, and Rsh is the sheet resistance of the semiconductor layer. Plotting RT against spacing and fitting a line gives Rsh/W as the slope and 2Rc as the y-intercept.

Cox & Strack — for circular contacts of diameter d, the total resistance follows

RT = 4Rc/(πd²) + 4tρ/(πd²)

where t and ρ are the thickness and resistivity of the diffused layer. Plotted against 1/d² instead of spacing, the slope of the fit gives Rc directly — this was the method I extended to solar cells, since it works on thinner semiconductors than TLM can measure.

The data points shown are illustrative, not the raw measurements from the thesis — but they follow the same equations and the same contact geometry as the Cox & Strack structure scene.
